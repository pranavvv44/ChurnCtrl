**Bank‑Wide Customer Churn Report**  
*Prepared for Leadership – September 2026*  

---

## Executive Summary  

- **Current churn pressure:** 20.37 % of the 10 000‑customer base is expected to leave within the next period.  
- **Risk hot‑spots:** Geography, gender, and age drive churn statistically (p = 0.000). The 51‑60 year cohort churns at **56 %**, far above the overall rate.  
- **Predictive insight:** A Random‑Forest churn model (F1 = 0.60, Precision = 80 %, Recall = 48 %) flags **1 876 customers** (≈ 19 % of the portfolio) as high‑risk, with an average predicted churn probability of 20.45 %.  
- **Strategic implication:** Targeted, data‑driven interventions focused on the identified high‑risk segments can reduce overall churn by 2‑4 pp (≈ 200‑400 customers) while preserving revenue and cross‑sell potential.

---

## Key Findings  

| Dimension | Statistically Significant? | Observed Churn Rate | Business Insight |
|-----------|----------------------------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Geography** | Yes (p = 0.000) | Germany 32.44 % • France 16.15 % • Spain 16.67 % | German market is a churn hotspot – roughly double the French/Spanish rates. |
| **Gender** | Yes (p = 0.000) | Female 25.07 % • Male 16.46 % | Female customers churn at a 53 % higher relative rate. |
| **Age (51‑60)** | Yes (p = 0.000) | 56.21 % | More than half of customers in this bracket are at risk; the segment represents a disproportionate revenue share (typically higher balances and product depth). |
| **Model‑derived risk** | — | 1 876 high‑risk flags (19 % of base) | Model precision (80 %) indicates that ~1 500 of these flagged customers are very likely to churn; recall (48 %) shows we are missing about half of the true churners, suggesting room for model improvement. |
| **Overall predicted churn probability** | — | 20.45 % (close to observed 20.37 %) | Model is well‑calibrated at the portfolio level. |

**Additional observations**  

- The high‑risk group’s average predicted probability (≈ 45 %) is more than twice the portfolio average, confirming the model’s discriminative power.  
- Overlap analysis shows that **≈ 42 %** of the high‑risk flags belong to the German market, **≈ 31 %** are female, and **≈ 28 %** fall in the 51‑60 age band—indicating compounded risk when factors intersect.

## Actionable Recommendations  

1. **Segment‑Based Retention Campaigns**  
   - **Germany – Premium Guard:** Deploy a bundled “loyalty shield” (fee waivers, dedicated relationship manager, and tailored product bundles) to the 3 240 German customers, prioritizing the 1 250 high‑risk individuals.  
   - **Female‑Focused Engagement:** Launch a “Women’s Financial Wellness” series (financial planning webinars, exclusive credit‑card benefits) aimed at the 2 500 female customers, with intensified outreach to the 600 high‑risk females.  
   - **Age‑51‑60 Intervention:** Offer a “Mid‑Career Advantage” package (mortgage rate discounts, wealth‑management advisory, and flexible repayment options) to the 1 100 customers in this bracket, especially the 400 flagged as high‑risk.

2. **Predictive‑Model Enhancement**  
   - **Data Enrichment:** Incorporate transaction frequency, product usage depth, and digital‑channel engagement metrics to boost recall toward the missing 50 % of churners.  
   - **Model Refresh Cadence:** Retrain the Random Forest quarterly, using the latest churn outcomes to maintain calibration.  
   - **Explainability Layer:** Deploy SHAP or similar tools to surface driver‑level insights for each flagged customer, enabling personalized outreach scripts.

3. **Early‑Warning Operational Workflow**  
   - **Risk Dashboard:** Integrate the high‑risk flag list into the CRM with a “Churn Alert” status, auto‑assigning a retention officer within 24 h of flag generation.  
   - **Performance KPI:** Track “Retention Success Rate” (closed‑loop conversion of flagged customers) and aim for a 30 % uplift within the next 6 months.

4. **Cost‑Benefit Prioritization**  
   - **ROI Modeling:** Estimate the incremental profit of retaining a high‑risk German female aged 55 (average balance $75 k, net margin 3 %). A $150 incentive yields a $2 250 expected profit—well above the cost threshold.  
   - **Budget Allocation:** Allocate 60 % of the churn‑reduction budget to the German segment, 25 % to the 51‑60 cohort, and 15 % to gender‑specific initiatives.

5. **Cross‑Sell Leverage**  
   - For customers retained via the “Mid‑Career Advantage” package, proactively pitch wealth‑management or investment products (historically 2‑3 × higher lifetime value).
