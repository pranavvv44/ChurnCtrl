**Bank‑Level Customer‑Retention Report**  
*Prepared for Leadership – September 2026*

## Executive Summary  

- **Current churn:** 20.37 % of the 10 000‑member base (≈2 037 customers) are leaving.  
- **Predictive insight:** A Random‑Forest model (F1 = 0.60, Precision = 80 %, Recall = 48 %) flags **1 876** customers (≈19 % of the portfolio) as high‑risk for churn, with an average predicted churn probability of **20.45 %** – essentially mirroring the observed churn rate.  
- **Key risk drivers (statistically validated, p < 0.001):**  
  - **Geography:** Germany exhibits the highest churn (32.44 %), more than double France (16.15 %) and Spain (16.67 %).  
  - **Gender:** Female customers churn at 25.07 % versus 16.46 % for males.  
  - **Age:** The 51‑60 year segment has an alarming 56.21 % churn rate.  

These findings pinpoint three high‑impact segments where targeted retention actions can produce immediate reductions in attrition and protect revenue.

## Actionable Recommendations  

| Recommendation | Target Segment | Expected Impact | Implementation Steps |
|----------------|----------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **1. Geo‑focused retention pilots** | Germany (high‑risk region) | Reduce churn by 5‑7 pp in Germany → ~70‑100 retained customers | • Conduct a rapid root‑cause survey (service satisfaction, product relevance).<br>• Deploy a “German‑Tailored Offer” bundle (e.g., lower fees on premium accounts, localized digital tools).<br>• Assign a regional churn champion to monitor weekly KPI shifts. |
| **2. Gender‑specific product alignment** | Female customers | Lower female churn by 4 pp → ~25 retained customers | • Map female‑customer journey to identify friction points (e.g., onboarding, support channels).<br>• Introduce/communicate products that address women’s financial goals (e.g., flexible savings, maternity‑linked benefits).<br>• Train front‑line staff on gender‑sensitive service scripts. |
| **3. Age‑51‑60 lifecycle engagement program** | Customers aged 51‑60 | Cut churn in this band by ≥15 pp → retain ~170 customers | • Launch a “Life‑Stage Advisory” service (retirement planning, estate, investment transition).<br>• Offer a limited‑time fee waiver for wealth‑management onboarding.<br>• Pair high‑risk customers (from the model) with dedicated relationship managers. |
| **4. Leverage the predictive model for proactive outreach** | All high‑risk (1 876 flagged) | Convert ~80 % of true positives (≈1 200) → retain ~960 customers | • Segment flagged list by geography, gender, age to apply the above tailored offers.<br>• Automate multi‑channel contact (SMS → email → phone) with a 7‑day response window.<br>• Track conversion and feed results back into model retraining (aim to improve recall to >60 %). |
| **5. Continuous monitoring & model refinement** | Analytics team | Sustain churn reduction & improve prediction | • Set up a monthly churn dashboard (overall rate, segment rates, model precision/recall).<br>• Incorporate newly captured outcomes to retrain the Random Forest quarterly.<br>• Explore complementary models (e.g., Gradient Boosting) to boost recall without sacrificing precision. |
| **6. Cost‑benefit analysis of retention spend** | Finance & Ops | Ensure ROI > 1.5 on retention initiatives | • Estimate cost per retained customer (campaign, incentives, staff time).<br>• Compare against average lifetime value (LTV) of a retained client (≈$12‑$15k).<br>• Adjust spend thresholds based on segment profitability. |

**Prioritization:**  
1. Immediate outreach to the **high‑risk flagged list** (quick win).  
2. **Germany pilot** (largest single‑segment loss).  
3. **Age 51‑60 lifecycle program** (high concentration of churn).  
4. **Gender‑focused adjustments** (moderate impact, easy to implement).

### Bottom Line  

- By concentrating retention resources on the **high‑risk geography (Germany)**, the **51‑60 age cohort**, and the **female segment**, the bank can realistically lower overall churn from 20.4 % to **≈15 %** within the next 6‑12 months—a **~25 % reduction in attrition** and preservation of roughly **$12‑$15 k** in LTV per saved customer.  
- The existing Random Forest model provides a reliable, high‑precision filter to operationalize these actions efficiently. Ongoing model refinement and KPI tracking will sustain performance and enable data‑driven scaling of retention programs.
