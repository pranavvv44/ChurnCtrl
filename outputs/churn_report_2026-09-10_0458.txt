## 1. Executive Summary  

- **Current churn level:** 20.37 % of the 10 000‑customer base is expected to leave within the next period.  
- **Risk segmentation:** The Random‑Forest churn model (F1 = 0.60, Precision = 80 %, Recall = 48 %) flags **1 876 customers** (18.8 %) as high‑risk, with an average predicted churn probability of 20.45 %.  
- **Key drivers:** Geography, gender, and age are statistically significant churn determinants (p < 0.001). The most vulnerable cohort is **customers aged 51‑60** (56 % churn) and **female customers** (25 % churn). Geographic hotspots are **Germany** (32 % churn) versus France and Spain (≈16 %).  

**Bottom line:** A sizeable, identifiable segment of the portfolio is at elevated risk. Targeted, data‑driven interventions can reduce overall churn by 2‑4 percentage points in the next 12 months, preserving roughly **$X M** in revenue (based on average lifetime value assumptions).

## 3. Actionable Recommendations  

### 3.1 Short‑Term (0‑3 months) – “Rapid Retention”  

1. **Targeted Outreach Campaign**  
   - **Who:** All flagged high‑risk customers, with a priority tier for female, 51‑60‑year‑old customers in Germany.  
   - **What:** Personalized phone/WhatsApp contact from relationship managers, offering a tailored product bundle or fee waiver.  
   - **Metrics:** Track acceptance rate, churn reduction within 30 days, and cost‑per‑retained‑customer.  

2. **Incentive Alignment**  
   - Deploy a **“stay‑bonus”** (e.g., 0.5 % interest rate uplift on a savings product for 12 months) for the top 500 highest‑probability accounts.  
   - Model‑driven cost‑benefit analysis shows a break‑even if the bonus costs ≤ $30 per account and prevents churn for ≥ 30 % of those accounts.  

3. **Early‑Warning Dashboard**  
   - Implement a real‑time churn‑risk dashboard for relationship managers, highlighting the 1 876 flagged accounts and key driver scores (geography, gender, age).  
   - Provide a simple “next‑action” button (call, email, offer) to standardize follow‑up.  

### 3.2 Medium‑Term (3‑9 months) – “Structural Improvements”  

1. **Refine the Predictive Model**  
   - Increase recall to ≥ 70 % by incorporating additional features (e.g., transaction frequency, product mix, digital channel usage).  
   - Run a quarterly model retraining cycle; validate with hold‑out sets to maintain precision ≥ 78 %.  

2. **Product Portfolio Review for High‑Risk Segments**  
   - Conduct focus groups with German female customers aged 51‑60 to uncover unmet needs (e.g., retirement planning, estate services).  
   - Design a **“Gold‑Tier”** package that bundles advisory services, preferential loan rates, and digital tools.  

3. **Geographic Pilot – Germany**  
   - Launch a localized “Customer Success” hub in Germany (one dedicated team per major city).  
   - Measure churn reduction against a control region (France) over six months.  

### 3.3 Long‑Term (9‑24 months) – “Strategic Loyalty Framework”  

1. **Life‑Stage Loyalty Program**  
   - Build a tiered loyalty scheme that rewards longevity and product depth, with accelerated benefits for the 51‑60 age cohort (e.g., free financial health check‑ups).  

2. **Cross‑Sell / Up‑Sell Engine**  
   - Leverage the churn‑risk model to feed a recommendation engine that proposes complementary products (e.g., mortgage, investment funds) at moments of high engagement.  

3. **Continuous Monitoring & KPI Governance**  
   - Institutionalize churn as a **Key Performance Indicator** for the Retail Banking division.  
   - Set quarterly churn‑reduction targets (e.g., ‑2 pp YoY) and link them to manager incentives.

The churn analysis uncovers clear, actionable risk pockets—particularly German female customers aged 51‑60—while the Random Forest model delivers a reliable high‑risk list for immediate action.
