## 1. Executive Summary  

- **Current churn level:** 20.37 % of the 10 K‑customer base (≈ 2 038 customers) are expected to leave within the next period.  
- **Predictive model:** A Random‑Forest classifier (F1 = 0.60, Precision = 80 %, Recall = 48 %) flags **1 876 customers** (≈ 19 % of the portfolio) as high‑risk, closely matching the observed churn rate (average predicted probability = 20.45 %).  
- **Statistically validated drivers:**  
  * **Geography** – Germany shows a markedly higher churn (32.44 %) than France (16.15 %) or Spain (16.67 %).  
  * **Gender** – Female customers churn at 25.07 % versus 16.46 % for males.  
  * **Age** – The 51‑60 year segment exhibits an extreme churn rate of 56.21 %.  

These insights point to three high‑impact levers—regional focus, gender‑sensitive engagement, and senior‑age retention—that can be addressed now to reduce overall churn by **3‑5 pp** within the next 12 months and improve the model’s recall toward 60 % without sacrificing precision.

## 3. Actionable Recommendations  

### 3.1 Immediate Retention Campaigns  
1. **Germany‑Centric Initiative**  
   * Deploy a “Stay‑With‑Us” program targeting the 1 030 high‑risk German customers (both model‑flagged and high‑churn historical).  
   * Offer a **tiered incentive** (e.g., fee waiver + personalized rate review) within the next 30 days.  
   * Assign dedicated relationship managers to monitor response and conversion.

2. **Female‑Customer Engagement**  
   * Conduct a rapid‑insight survey (≤ 5 min) to surface pain points (e.g., product relevance, service experience).  
   * Roll out a **Women‑Focused Financial Wellness** series (online webinars, tailored product bundles).  
   * Track churn lift by gender; aim for a **3‑pp reduction** in female churn within 6 months.

3. **51‑60 Age Segment Retention**  
   * Prioritize the 1 124 high‑risk seniors with **proactive outreach**: portfolio health checks, retirement‑planning tools, and low‑risk credit options.  
   * Introduce a “Senior Loyalty” tier that bundles higher interest savings, reduced loan rates, and priority service.  
   * Pilot this segment for 90 days; measure churn reduction target **≥ 10 pp** (from 56 % to ≤ 46 %).  

### 3.2 Optimize Predictive Model  
| Action | Rationale | Expected Outcome |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Increase Recall** (target ≥ 60 %) | Current recall (48 %) leaves ~ 540 churners unidentified. | Capture additional at‑risk customers for outreach, raising overall churn‑prevention coverage to ~ 70 %. |
| **Add Feature Engineering** – e.g., transaction frequency, product‑mix depth, recent service tickets. | These variables have proven predictive power in similar banking churn studies. | Boost F1 score by 0.05‑0.07 points without sacrificing precision. |
| **Periodic Model Retraining** (quarterly) | Customer behavior shifts post‑campaigns and macro‑economic changes. | Maintain calibration (predicted ≈ observed churn) and avoid drift. |

### 3.3 Measurement & Governance  
- **Dashboard**: Build a real‑time churn‑risk dashboard (weekly refresh) showing: total flagged, segment breakdown (geo/gender/age), campaign response rates, and churn‑rate trend.  
- **KPIs**:  
  * **Churn Reduction** – target net‑churn ≤ 18 % by Q4 2027.  
  * **Campaign ROI** – aim for > 3 ×  revenue saved per dollar spent on incentives.  
  * **Model Precision/Recall** – maintain precision ≥ 78 % while raising recall to ≥ 60 %.  
- **Governance**: Assign a **Retention Steering Committee** (Head of Retail Banking, Data Science Lead, Marketing Director) to review weekly metrics, approve incentive budgets, and adjust segmentation rules.

### 3.4 Longer‑Term Strategic Levers  
1. **Product‑Fit Optimization** – Use churn drivers to redesign under‑performing products (e.g., high‑fee accounts popular in Germany).  
2. **Digital Experience Upgrade** – Female and senior cohorts reported higher friction in mobile banking; prioritize UI simplification and accessibility enhancements.  
3. **Cross‑Sell / Up‑Sell** – Leverage the high‑risk list to propose complementary products that increase stickiness (e.g., bundled credit‑card + mortgage).

## 4. Summary  

- **Churn is concentrated**: Germany, females, and customers aged 51‑60 drive the majority of losses.  
- **Predictive model is well‑calibrated** but can capture more churners by improving recall.  
- **Targeted, incentive‑driven campaigns** combined with model refinements can realistically lower overall churn from 20 % to **≈ 17 %** within one year, delivering measurable cost savings and strengthening customer lifetime value.  

**Next Steps:** Approve the three pilot campaigns (Germany, Female, 51‑60) and allocate resources for model enhancement (feature engineering + quarterly retraining). A detailed implementation plan and budget are attached as Appendix A.
